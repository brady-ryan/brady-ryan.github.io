What you’re looking at

A massive object sits between you and a background light source. Its gravity deflects passing light, so the source appears displaced, distorted and sometimes copied. Surface brightness is preserved, so stretched images also look larger and brighter overall.

Critical curves

The gold line marks where magnification becomes formally infinite. When a source’s image touches it, two images merge or split apart. For a single, perfectly aligned lens it is the Einstein ring itself.

Magnification map

Blue regions show images with the source’s own orientation; orange regions show mirror-flipped images. Brighter tint means stronger magnification.
